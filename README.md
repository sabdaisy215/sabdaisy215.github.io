            <h1>Roguelikes:</h1>
            
            <h2>What is a Roguelike?</h2>
            <p>
                A Roguelike is a genre of videogames defined by the 
                randomization of each run. You begin a game, go in, die, and do it all 
                again with slight differences in the world around you or items you can 
                find. It's one of the hottest current genres being pushed out largely by
                indie studios. It's marked by the idea of 'runs' 
                and metaprogress, giving the player a reason to keep playing.
            </p>

            <h2>Good VS Bad Roguelikes</h2>
            <p>
                A good Roguelike will make you feel like you're playing chess—thinking of
                future options, possibilities, synergies, and fights to build toward the climax. 
                The repetition is fun and rewarding rather than frustrating. Players in a 
                good roguelike are more likely to treat every failure as a lesson.
            </p>

            <h2>Essential Mechanics</h2>
            <ul class="mechanics-list">
                <li>Procedural Generation</li>
                <li>High-stakes combat</li>
                <li>Losing as a learning experience</li>
                <li>Meaningful Metaprogression</li>
                <li>Sustaining Replayability</li>
                <li>Skill outweighs luck</li>
            </ul>
